## 1. Orbital launch and space transportation
- **CONFIRMED / operations + demand.** A Falcon 9 launched Starlink Group 15-24 from Vandenberg SLC-4E at 14:26 UTC on 6 September (7:26 a.m. PDT). Payload was 27 Starlink satellites. Booster B1088 flew its 19th mission and landed on the droneship *Of Course I Still Love You* about 8.5 minutes after liftoff; the upper stage deployed the stack on schedule at T+62 minutes. Space.com counted it as the 103rd Falcon 9 of 2026 and the 80th Starlink mission of the year. Florida Falcon 9 Starlink flights have been shifted to Vandenberg pending Starship operations on the Space Coast. Why it matters: West-Coast cadence is now the entire Falcon Starlink pipe; the medium-lift peer set (New Glenn, Vulcan) remains grounded, so constellation replenishment still runs through one operational reusable workhorse. [Space.com, 6 Sep](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/spacex-starlink/group-15-24-launch-ocisly); [SpaceX mission page](https://www.spacex.com/launches/sl-15-24); [Spaceflight Now live coverage, 5–6 Sep](https://spaceflightnow.com/2026/09/05/live-coverage-spacex-to-launch-80th-starlink-mission-of-2026/).
- **REPORTED / capital + competitors + demand.** La Tribune (7 September, 01:00 CEST) said ArianeGroup must decide quickly whether to raise Ariane 6 cadence above the 9–10 launches per year cruise rate. ESA Director General Josef Aschbacher has warned of a European launcher bottleneck around 2029–31 if constellation and institutional demand land as forecast. The first International Space Summit opens in Paris on 9–10 September at the Grand Palais, with about 120 delegations invited; CNES previewed the program on 7 September. Why it matters: Europe’s only heavy-lift line is capacity-constrained while Amazon Leo still holds 18 Ariane slots and both New Glenn and ULA Vulcan stay down. [La Tribune, 7 Sep](https://www.latribune.fr/article/defense-aerospatiale/spatial/13329485494182/ariane-6-arianegroup-contraint-de-prendre-tres-vite-une-decision-sur-une-hausse-des-cadences-de-lancements); [Reuters via CNA, 2 Sep backdrop](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/business/europe-may-accelerate-space-launches-meet-satellite-demand-6358006); [CNES summit preview, 7 Sep](https://cnes.fr/actualites/sommet-international-lespace-communaute-spatiale-mondiale-reunie-paris).

## 9. Frontier AI models, assistants, and AI developer platforms
- **CONFIRMED / technology + competitors.** Anthropic published a machine-checked Lean 4 formalization of Fermat’s Last Theorem produced by a swarm of Claude agents over 11 days: about 13 million lines and roughly 29,500 of 30,300 intermediate theorems, using only Lean’s three standard axioms. The repository (github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem, Apache 2.0) follows the Darmon–Diamond–Taylor exposition of Wiles/Taylor. Imperial College’s Kevin Buzzard said it “faithfully follows the early literature and adds nothing” to the mathematics — the news is autonomous formalization scale, not a new proof. New Scientist dated 5 September; The Next Web and Heise circulated 6 September. Why it matters: first public demo that a frontier lab’s agent swarm can finish a years-scale formal-math project in days, sitting next to gated GPT-6 Astra (3 September) rather than replacing it. [New Scientist, 5 Sep](https://www.newscientist.com/article/2587839-fermats-last-theorem-formalised-by-ai-agents-in-just-11-days/); [The Next Web, 6 Sep](https://thenextweb.com/news/anthropic-claude-fermat-last-theorem-lean-buzzard); [GitHub repo](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem).
