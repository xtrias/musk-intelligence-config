## 1. Orbital launch and space transportation

- **CONFIRMED / technology + competitors.** Galactic Energy flew Pallas-1 Y1 to orbit at 02:00 UTC on 1 September from the Dongfeng commercial pad at Jiuquan. The ~52 m, ~283 t kerolox vehicle uses a seven-engine first-stage cluster; the demo carried no operational payload and made no recovery attempt. Future flights are planned to attempt first-stage vertical landing. Why it matters: China now has another private medium-lift liquid vehicle in the reusable peer set against Falcon 9, LandSpace Zhuque-3, grounded New Glenn, and unflown Neutron. [SpaceNews China desk, 1 Sep](https://spacenews.com/tag/china/); [Global Times, 1 Sep](https://www.globaltimes.cn/page/202609/1369524.shtml).
- **CONFIRMED / capital + competitors.** NASA awarded Blue Origin a firm-fixed-price contract (C26-010) with a maximum potential value of about $700 million to design, build, launch and operate a Mars Telecommunications Orbiter, delivery no later than 31 December 2028 and operations targeted around 2030. Why it matters: the award extends Blue Origin beyond Earth-orbit launch and lunar landers into deep-space communications infrastructure while New Glenn itself remains grounded after the 28 May pad explosion. [NASA C26-010, 1 Sep](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-selects-blue-origin-as-mars-telecommunications-network-provider/).
- **CONFIRMED / supply chain.** Blue Origin and the US Space Force broke ground on a ~120,000 sq ft payload processing facility at former SLC-18, Cape Canaveral SFS — four processing bays plus two encapsulation bays, sized for about 16 missions a year, targeted open early 2028. Florida Today put the project at about $160 million, split roughly evenly with USSF. Why it matters: NSSL and commercial cadence infrastructure is being built while Vulcan (grounded since the 12 February GEM-63XL anomaly) and New Glenn are both still down. [Aviation Week, 1 Sep](https://aviationweek.com/space/commercial-space/blue-origin-breaks-ground-payload-processing-plant); [Florida Today, 31 Aug / covered 1 Sep](https://www.floridatoday.com/story/tech/science/space/2026/08/31/blue-origin-job-growth-surges-despite-new-glenn-rocket-launch-pad-blast-in-brevard-county-florida/91544486007/).
- **REPORTED / operations.** Falcon 9 Starlink Group 15-23 (27 satellites, Vandenberg SLC-4E) was on the 2 September morning window. Rocket Lab Electron “Owl Around The World” (Synspective StriX SAR, Mahia LC-1A) remained NET 2 September after an 31 August wind slip. Why it matters: small-lift and West Coast Falcon cadence are the only US commercial launch lines flying while two medium/heavy competitors sit grounded. [KEYT, 1 Sep](https://keyt.com/vandenberg-space-base/2026/09/01/falcon-9-launch-of-starlink-satellites-scheduled-for-early-wednesday-morning/).

Watch next: whether Starlink 15-23 and Electron both clear 2 September; any dated Vulcan LV-01 or New Glenn return-to-flight statement; Isar Spectrum’s early-September Andøya window.

## 3. Electric vehicles and charging networks
