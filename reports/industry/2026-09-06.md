## 1. Orbital launch and space transportation
- **CONFIRMED / competitors + technology + demand.** Isar Aerospace’s Spectrum Flight 2 (“Onward and Upward”) lifted off Andøya Spaceport, Norway, at 20:12 UTC on 5 September and reached orbit. First private orbital launch from continental / Western European soil. Two-stage, 28 m vehicle; nine Aquila first-stage engines plus one vacuum Aquila; propane/LOX. Initial orbit about 500 × 180 km, then circularized with a second-stage restart. Exolaunch deployed five CubeSats (CyBEEsat / TU Berlin; TriSat-S / University of Maribor; Platform 6 / EnduroSat; FramSat-1 / NTNU; SpaceTeamSat1 / TU Wien) plus a non-separable Dcubed actuator experiment under ESA Boost!. Maiden flight on 30 March 2025 failed seconds after liftoff; 2026 attempts were scrubbed for weather, a range-intruding boat, a COPV leak and fluid-system issues, including a 4 September wind stand-down. Why it matters: Europe now has a demonstrated commercial small-lift alternative to Rocket Lab Electron and to Kourou-only Ariane/Vega slots; Isar cites roughly 1,000 kg LEO capacity and a Munich plant sized for 30–40 vehicles/year. Reuters, Bloomberg, SpaceNews, Space.com, Aviation Week and ESA all dated the success 5–6 September. [Reuters, 5 Sep](https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/german-space-rocket-lifts-off-norway-base-2026-09-05/); [Bloomberg, 5 Sep](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-05/german-startup-isar-s-rocket-lifts-off-on-european-space-mission); [SpaceNews, 5 Sep](https://spacenews.com/isar-aerospace-reaches-orbit-on-second-spectrum-launch/); [Space.com, 5 Sep](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/isar-aerospace-second-launch-norway-andoya-spaceport-spectrum-rocket); [Aviation Week, 5 Sep](https://aviationweek.com/space/launch-vehicles-propulsion/isar-aerospaces-spectrum-rocket-reaches-orbit-european-milestone).
- **CONFIRMED / technology + operations (servicing).** SpaceNews reported 5 September that Katalyst Space is wrapping LINK operations after NASA scaled the Swift reboost to a technology demonstration. LINK launched 3 July on a Northrop Grumman Pegasus XL from Kwajalein under a $30 million NASA SBIR Phase III. Attitude-control failures blocked docking. In remaining time Katalyst deployed all three robotic arms, fired all three xenon electric-propulsion thrusters together, and closed to about 12–15 km of Swift; no closer approach planned; deorbit in about two to three weeks. Why it matters: the first US commercial attempt to grapple and reboost an unprepared NASA science asset failed on the capture, but generated rendezvous-and-proximity-operations data as Swift’s orbit continues decaying toward a late-2026 reentry. [SpaceNews, 5 Sep](https://spacenews.com/katalyst-space-nears-end-of-attempted-swift-reboost-mission/).
- **CONFIRMED / technology + competitors (coverage dated in-window).** SpaceNews (6 September piece, 5 September byline) and Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory described results from the Space Force Victus Haze exercise: Rocket Lab’s Puma spacecraft used an LLNL monolithic-silica optical payload to track True Anomaly’s Jackal. Electron launched 19 June from Māhia 16 hours 42 minutes after notice-to-launch. Why it matters: published evidence that commercially built smallsats plus laboratory optics can do on-orbit inspection on tactically responsive timelines — a competitor capability in responsive launch and space-domain awareness, not a new liftoff. [SpaceNews, 6 Sep](https://spacenews.com/lawrence-livermore-telescope-used-in-space-force-cat-and-mouse-exercise/).
- **CONFIRMED / regulation + operations (no delta).** FAA/AST posted no new Part 450 license modification in-window after checking commercial-space pages and the Spaceflight Now log. New Glenn remains grounded after the 28 May LC-36 pad explosion; ULA Vulcan LV-01 remains NET September pending GEM-63XL / shared BE-4 valve closeout. Next dated US commercial medium-lift on the public schedule is Falcon 9 Starlink 15-24 from Vandenberg SLC-4E, window opening about 10:59–11:13 UTC on 6 September (after this brief’s cutoff). [SpaceX mission page](https://www.spacex.com/launches/sl-15-24); [Spaceflight Now, 5 Sep](https://spaceflightnow.com/2026/09/05/live-coverage-spacex-to-launch-80th-starlink-mission-of-2026/).

## 9. Frontier AI models, assistants, and AI developer platforms
