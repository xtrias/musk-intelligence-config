## 2. Satellite broadband and direct-to-device connectivity
- **CONFIRMED / regulation + technology.** FCC Chairman Brendan Carr on 8 September announced two September Open Meeting items: a satellite “spectrum abundance” order that would unlock more than 1,000 MHz in the 12.7 GHz and 42 GHz bands for home satellite broadband, in-flight and maritime connectivity, and gateway routing; and an FNPRM exploring a further 1,450 MHz in Ku-/Ka-band plus 138.25 GHz in D-band, including spectrum for in-orbit servicing and other non-consumer space missions. A companion item would cut NEPA-style permitting on a range of FCC-regulated space activities. Why it matters: the second-constellation problem is now as much a spectrum-and-siting problem as a launcher problem. [FCC news release, 8 Sep](https://www.fcc.gov/document/fcc-unlock-more-spectrum-next-gen-connectivity); [FCC fact sheet DOC-424775A1](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-424775A1.txt); [FCC headlines, 8 Sep](https://www.fcc.gov/press-resources).
- **CONFIRMED / regulation.** The Federal Register on 8 September published the FCC’s NPRM *Unleashing Unlicensed Spectrum for Direct-to-Device* (ET Docket 26-169; FCC 26-51), proposing to let Part 15 devices communicate with authorized satellites on a non-interference basis in 2400–2483.5 MHz and 5725–5850 MHz and to clarify Part 15 gear aboard spacecraft. Comments due 9 November; replies 7 December. Adopted 6 August; the Register notice starts the clock. Why it matters: this is an industry-wide D2D framework, not a single-operator grant, and it sits alongside Amazon Leo’s 5,105-satellite D2D application (comments still due 17 September). [Federal Register, 8 Sep](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/08/2026-18282/unleashing-unlicensed-spectrum-for-direct-to-device).
- **REPORTED / competitors + demand.** AT&T said on 8 September it had signed to deliver Amazon Leo satellite broadband to US business customers — the first US telecom reseller named for Leo. Amazon still lists enterprise preview now and commercial service later in 2026. Jonathan McDowell’s tracker: ~392 Leo satellites in space, ~315 in operational orbit; Wedbush has cited ~578 as a commercial-service threshold. Last dedicated Leo launch remains ULA Atlas V on 2 July; Vulcan LV-01 is still the next dedicated ride and is undated. Why it matters: distribution is being signed before the constellation is at the published service density. [MarketWatch / Dow Jones, 8 Sep](https://www.morningstar.com/news/marketwatch/2026090851/amazon-and-att-team-up-to-challenge-spacexs-satellite-dominance); [Quartz, 8 Sep](https://qz.com/amazon-att-satellite-internet-spacex-starlink-090826).
