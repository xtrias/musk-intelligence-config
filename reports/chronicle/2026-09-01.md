### Tesla — vehicles, FSD, Europe
**CONFIRMED.** Reuters, 1 Sep: August new-vehicle registrations, a sales proxy, split sharply across Europe. France +279% year-on-year and Denmark +104% (PFA; bilstatistik.dk). Norway −79% and Sweden −41% (OFV; Mobility Sweden). An updated Reuters write-through added Spain −79% (ANFAC; 304 cars). Britain and Germany, the two largest markets, report later this week. Rico Luman of ING Research attributed the France/Denmark rebound to EV adoption and Tesla price cuts, while noting Chinese brands keep expanding the competitive set. French outlet coverage and Best Selling Cars Blog put Model Y first among all cars in France in August (4,622 units; YTD 29,658). Operationally this is volume allocation, not a continent-wide recovery: Tesla is still growing where incentives are rich and shrinking where they are not.

https://www.reuters.com/business/autos-transportation/teslas-european-registrations-mixed-august-with-strong-france-denmark-supporting-2026-09-01/

**CONFIRMED.** Official @teslaeurope, 07:40 CEST 1 Sep: FSD Supervised is approved in five EU states — Netherlands, Denmark, Belgium, Estonia, Lithuania — used by more than 70,000 customers for over 1 million km per day. Tesla says vehicles on FSD were 4.1× less likely to crash than Tesla manual driving across 100 million km on public EU roads. The company published the safety dashboard previously given to EU member states after the Dutch RDW approval, ahead of a possible EU-wide Technical Committee on Motor Vehicles vote on 6 October 2026. Design was adjusted to UN-R-171 DCAS; Tesla sought Article 39 exemptions for some behaviors. The 4.1× figure is Tesla’s own comparison against its manual-driven fleet, not an independent regulator study. Independent trackers put current coverage at 5 of 27 states and about 8.9% of EU population; EU-wide recognition needs 15 states and 65% of population.

https://x.com/teslaeurope/status/2094661569582096779

**CONFIRMED.** Reuters, Sydney, 1 Sep: Federal Court judge Tom Thawley said he may appoint an independent expert with access to Tesla’s internal engineering platforms in Hansen v Tesla Motors Australia (NSD227/2025), a class action for about 10,000 Model 3 and Model Y owners alleging phantom braking and overstated self-driving and range claims. Plaintiffs say document discovery has missed core technical systems. Tesla counsel called those systems “highly confidential.” Both sides will take instructions; next listing 12 November. A live-system expert would be an unusual discovery shortcut if ordered.

https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/australian-judge-floats-giving-court-appointed-expert-access-teslas-engineering-2026-09-01/

### SpaceX / Starlink — launch and constellation
**REPORTED (FCC filing).** SpaceX / Space Exploration Holdings filed at the FCC on 31 August seeking temporary 60-day authority in 42.0–42.5 GHz for new high-capacity Starlink satellites, with a planned deployment date of 15 September 2026 on Starship Flight 14. StarshipGazer and LabPadre read the filing as the first orbital Starship attempt and the first operational (not suborbital demo) V3 deployment. Benzinga and SpaceX France independently described the same date. SpaceX has not posted an official launch date. Flight 13 (24 July) deployed 20 V3s on a suborbital profile that then burned up. V3 design claim, from prior company material: ~1 Tbps downlink per satellite; a full Starship load is described as up to 60.

**Context, just outside the window.** Falcon Heavy launched NASA’s Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope from LC-39A at 07:26 EDT Sunday 30 August. Side boosters landed at LZ-2 and LZ-40. @SpaceX posted landing photos early Monday 31 August (01:13 GMT), before this chronicle’s 24h cut. Roman is en route to Sun–Earth L2 for a three-month commissioning; first images targeted early 2027. Flight 14 booster B21 completed a full-duration 33-engine static fire on 28 August.

No Falcon 9 / Starlink launch occurred in the last 24 hours after checking Spaceflight Now and Space.com calendars.
