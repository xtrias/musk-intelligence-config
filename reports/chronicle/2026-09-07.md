### Electric vehicles — Tesla China cash cuts (CONFIRMED, 7 Sep)
Tesla China posted on Weibo Monday that customers who order and take delivery of inventory Model 3 or Model Y (including Model Y L) by 30 September 2026 receive a cash cut on the final payment: ¥5,000 on all inventory Model 3 variants and ¥10,000 on all inventory Model Y / Y L variants. CnEVPost and the South China Morning Post both treated the Weibo post as a company announcement. SCMP called it Tesla China’s first cash-price cut on Shanghai-built cars since late 2024.

The cash cuts stack with existing limited-time paint benefits (¥8,000 on selected configurations), an ¥8,000 insurance subsidy on non-Performance Model 3 variants, and five-year 0% financing on eligible Model 3s with a ¥79,900 down payment. After the stack, Tesla China marketing creatives show Model 3 from ¥222,500 and Model Y from ¥253,500. Eligibility is inventory vehicles, not built-to-order.

Why it matters operationally: China retail remains the weak leg of Giga Shanghai. July China retail was 27,249 vehicles (−32.91% year-on-year). August wholesale was 86,166 (+3.57% y/y, −7.92% vs July), and January–August wholesale was 647,694 (+25.63% y/y) — a series that includes exports. SCMP quoted Shanghai consultancy Suolei manager Eric Han saying Tesla had previously preferred insurance subsidies and interest-free loans over list-price cuts, and that a cash cut “is likely to trigger a fresh round of price competition amid weak consumer demand.” A ¥5,000 Model 3 cut is about 2.1% off the ¥235,500 base list; a ¥10,000 Model Y cut is about 3.8% off the ¥263,500 entry Y.

### Autonomy / robotaxi — NHTSA Cybercab audit still the US regulatory fact (CONFIRMED document 3 Sep; 7 Sep recaps)
NHTSA Open Audit Query AQ26002, opened 3 September 2026, remains the binding US document. Investigator Syed Rahaman; estimated population about 1,000 Cybercabs. It is not a crash investigation. The Office of Vehicle Safety Compliance is auditing the process and technical data Tesla used to self-certify a two-seat vehicle with no permanently attached steering wheel, pedals, or mirrors under Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards. Tesla told the agency the Cybercab meets “all applicable” FMVSS; NHTSA said it will examine how far that claim rests on determinations that certain standards are inapplicable.

Commercial Cybercab rides in limited Austin geofences began 3–4 September. Texas registration figures cited by Reuters / TechCrunch / Not a Tesla App: about 420 autonomous vehicles registered in Texas, including 45 Cybercabs; the rest are modified Model Ys that still have conventional controls. Zoox previously used a Part 555 exemption path for a comparable no-controls design; Tesla used ordinary self-certification.

7 September recaps: Motor1, Automotive World, Technology.org. Tesla stock last cash close Friday 4 September $354.08 (−5.92%, volume ~65 million) after the muted launch and the audit. Nasdaq closed Labor Day Monday.
