### Tesla — Cybercab public rides and NHTSA audit
**CONFIRMED.** NHTSA Office of Defects Investigation opened Audit Query **AQ26002** on 3 Sep 2026, made public Friday 4 Sep. Population: an estimated **1,000** Cybercab vehicles. Subject: Tesla Cybercab FMVSS certification. Investigator Syed Rahaman; reviewer Neil Dold; approver Otto Matheke. Prompted by public information.

Tesla notified the agency it had certified the vehicles as compliant with all applicable Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards and that it plans to expand commercial deployment gradually to more vehicles and locations. The filing states the vehicles lack permanently attached conventional manual controls — brake pedal, gas pedal, steering wheel, and mirrors. NHTSA will examine the process and technical data behind that certification and “the extent to which Tesla’s certification depended on determinations that certain FMVSS are inapplicable to the Cybercab.”

NHTSA Administrator Jonathan Morrison: “NHTSA fully supports the safe development and deployment of automated vehicles. But as the federal regulator, we need to ensure that all of our laws are followed.” Existing FMVSS remain in force; the AV rule rewrite is not finished.

Primary documents: [NHTSA press release](https://www.nhtsa.gov/press-releases/investigation-tesla-cybercab-self-certification); [AQ26002 resume PDF](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/inv/2026/INOA-AQ26002-17078.pdf). Wires: [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/autos-transportation/us-auto-safety-regulator-opens-probe-into-nearly-1000-tesla-cybercabs-2026-09-04/), [Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/09/04/tesla-cybercab-nhtsa-investigation-fmvss-certification/), [Teslarati](https://www.teslarati.com/tesla-cybercab-launch-nhtsa-attention-know-more/), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/04/feds-launch-investigation-into-teslas-cybercab-deployment/), [POLITICO](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/09/04/trump-cybercab-musk-probe-01064706).

### X
**CONFIRMED court order, coverage through Friday–Saturday.** D. Del. Chief Judge Colm F. Connolly, *X Corp. v. Operation Bluebird, Inc.*, 1:25-cv-01510. Memorandum opinion and order 3 Sep; X posted a $500,000 surety bond statement 4 Sep.

- **GRANTED:** preliminary injunction blocking Operation Bluebird from using Twitter-formative word marks for a social network (X still says “formerly known as Twitter” in the Apple App Store listing).
- **DENIED:** injunction as to the “Tweet” mark and the bird logo. Connolly found Bluebird likely to prove X discontinued bona fide use and does not intend to resume — citing Musk’s “bid adieu to the Twitter brand” and blow-torch-the-logo remarks.
- Bluebird rebranded to Tweet.app and launched. Bond $500,000. Ruling is preliminary, not a final judgment.

([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/x-corp-blocks-social-media-startup-using-twitter-name-2026-09-03/); [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/09/musk-wins-court-order-to-block-use-of-twitter-but-not-tweet-and-bird-logo/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/04/judge-blocks-x-rival-from-using-twitter-name-but-allows-tweet-for-now/); [order PDF](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.ded.91484/gov.uscourts.ded.91484.96.0_1.pdf))
