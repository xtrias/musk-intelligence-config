### SpaceX — CFO at Goldman; Flight 14 framed as first revenue Starship
**CONFIRMED.** SpaceX CFO Bret Johnsen spoke at the Goldman Sachs Communacopia + Technology Conference on Thursday 10 Sep at 12:25 p.m. PT (21:25 CEST, inside the window). SpaceX posted the IR webcast; Goldman Sachs streamed it. Coverage landed Friday from Barron’s, Aviation Week, SpaceNews, Benzinga and Data Center Dynamics.

Johnsen’s operational points, as reported by those outlets (Seeking Alpha has a transcript; the body is paywalled):

- Starship Flight 14 is expected later this month and is the first flight he described as **revenue-generating**, carrying production Starlink V3 satellites to operational orbit. Flight 13’s ~20 V3s in July were a suborbital demonstration. Hardware pairing in public trackers is Super Heavy B21 + Ship 41 (Block 3). A ship catch is not the baseline for this flight.
- He restated conviction that SpaceX can annualize a **$100 billion ARR** rate from the December 2026 month.
- SpaceX signed another terrestrial AI-compute hosting contract with an undisclosed customer. Conference recaps (Benzinga / Yahoo Finance) quote Johnsen: “just earlier this month we closed another hosting deal, and that translates into about $1.11 billion a month starting December 1st of this year, which is another roughly $13 billion of ARR.” William Blair’s Louie DiPalma said this lease is distinct from a $6.7 billion / six-month deal discussed on the August earnings call. Data Center Dynamics called the new contract the fourth large lease after previously reported Anthropic, Google and Reflection contracts; stacked totals are DCD arithmetic, not a SpaceX figure. Recaps of the same talk put year-end terrestrial compute a little above 2 GW, with a $30–50 per watt revenue sketch for next year — conference color, not a 10-K line.
- On China: an “amazing space program,” but “not on a Starship level anytime soon,” per Benzinga’s readout.
- Orbital compute (“Starmind”) satellites “as soon as 2027”; Johnsen floated that reuse could make orbital compute cheaper than terrestrial “maybe as soon as next year.”

Why it matters: this is the first time a SpaceX officer has put a dollar label on a Starship mission in public, and it ties V3 mass-to-orbit to the listed-company ARR story rather than to a test-flight narrative.

**CONFIRMED (official wrap in-window).** SpaceX posted Thursday 21:01 UTC that “Falcon 9 launches USSF-153 to orbit from pad 4E in California.” Company page: 8:42 a.m. PT 10 Sep, SLC-4E Vandenberg; first stage landed on droneship *Of Course I Still Love You*. KeepTrack: fifth U.S. national-security mission of 2026; booster B1081 on flight 27. Payload remains classified. Barron’s/Dow Jones noted the mission as the 104th Falcon 9 of 2026 (106th flight including Starship tests) and that Falcon 9’s share of global launches is running near 49% versus more than 50% and 165 Falcon 9 flights in 2025. The liftoff itself was mid-afternoon CEST on 10 Sep (just before the window); the “to orbit” post, landing video and Friday stock/CFO coverage sit inside it.

### Tesla — Semi Europe specs go live; FSD misuse letter; China Performance SKU
**CONFIRMED.** Tesla Europe posted Friday that “Semi is coming to Europe,” and Reuters (Prathik Jayaprakash, Akash Sriram, 16:48 UTC) reported the company will give European specifications and launch details at IAA Transportation in Hanover next week. A German product page is live.

Published Euro specs (Tesla Europe / tesla.com/de_de/semi; Not a Tesla App, Electrek, autoevolution):

- Up to 550 km range at 40-tonne gross combination weight, fully loaded
- About 1.0 kWh/km
- Megacharger up to 800 kW; ~60% of range in 30 minutes; MCS 3.2
- Three rear motors, up to 800 kW drive power
- Curb weight 9,100 kg; ePTO up to 25 kW
- Camera-monitor side views instead of physical mirrors (legal on EU trucks since 2016; FMVSS 111 still requires mirrors in the U.S.)

Not a Tesla App and VRT put **customer deliveries in 2027**. Reuters notes the July shareholder update said Semi production would “begin in 2026” and dropped an earlier volume-production-this-year forecast. Nevada’s high-volume line has been running since April; limited U.S. deliveries began in late 2022 (PepsiCo among early customers). The Euro truck matches the U.S. Standard Range package; the U.S. Long Range pitch (about 500 miles, peak Megacharger rates listed as high as 1.2 MW) is not the one published for Europe.
