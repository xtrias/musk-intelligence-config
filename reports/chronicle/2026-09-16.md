### Autonomy / regulation — NHTSA Special Order on Cybercab
**CONFIRMED.** Reuters reported 15 September that the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration ordered Tesla to answer, by 30 September, a series of questions on whether it properly self-certified the Cybercab robotaxi. The underlying instrument is a Special Order inside Audit Query AQ26002, opened after Tesla began commercial two-seater Cybercab service in Austin on 3 September. The PDF (NHTSA ODI INIM-AQ26002-17090P1) lists 21 requests signed by Chief Counsel Peter Simshauser. Electrek (15 September) and InsideEVs (16 September) published the same document.

NHTSA notes the Cybercab lacks a steering wheel, brake pedal, accelerator pedal and mirrors. Request 19 points at FMVSS No. 135 S5.3.1 — “The service brakes shall be activated by means of a foot control” — and asks how Tesla certified compliance. Other requests cover temporary human-driver controls, the Safety Act “make inoperative” prohibition, FMVSS 101/102/108/111/126, and why Tesla certified without a Part 555 exemption. Amazon’s Zoox obtained a limited exemption; Tesla self-certified. Electrek and Automotive World state that an incomplete or false sworn response can carry civil penalties of up to $27,874 per day, capped at about $139 million, plus criminal exposure for a signing officer. Those penalty figures are the outlets’ reading of the statute, not a fine already levied.

Operationally this is the legal gate on selling or expanding a wheel-less Cybercab beyond the current Austin geo-fence. NHTSA proposed in June to drop the foot-brake requirement for automated vehicles; that rewrite is not final. Tesla has not published a response. Sources: Reuters; NHTSA PDF; Electrek; InsideEVs.

### Chip manufacturing — Tesla and SpaceX sue over the “Terafab” name
**CONFIRMED.** Reuters (16 September) reported Tesla and SpaceX filed in the Western District of Texas (Austin) seeking a declaration that “Terafab” does not infringe Illinois nanotech firm TERA-print LLC’s “Tera-Fab” trademark. TERA-print sent a cease-and-desist in June and threatened suit; Tesla filed three USPTO “Terafab” applications in May. Complaint quote, per Reuters: “No reasonable consumer is likely to believe that Plaintiffs’ planned Terafab chip factory is sponsored by, affiliated with, or connected to TERA-print’s desktop pen lithography products.” The filing restates the previously announced Grimes County plant: make, package and test advanced logic and memory under one roof for Optimus, Cybercab and SpaceX orbital compute; initial investment $16.8 billion. Counsel named by Reuters: Andrei Iancu (Sullivan & Cromwell) for Tesla; Megan Bannigan (Debevoise) for SpaceX. This is a trademark fight, not a new construction permit. Sources: Reuters.
