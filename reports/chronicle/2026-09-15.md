### Orbital launch — Starship Flight 14 dated
**CONFIRMED.** SpaceX said Tuesday that Starship Flight 14 is targeting launch as early as Tuesday, 22 September 2026, pending regulatory approval. The official mission page lists a 75-minute window opening 07:15 CT / 08:15 ET. This is planned as the first orbital Starship flight after 13 suborbital tests. Ship 41 on Super Heavy is expected to fly about 275 km altitude, complete about six orbits over a nearly 10-hour mission, then splash down in the Pacific west of Chile. The booster’s test objectives are launch, ascent, stage separation, boostback and a landing burn at an offshore point in the Gulf of America — not a tower catch. SpaceX wrote: “By going to orbit, the next phase of developing Starship to be fully and rapidly reusable can begin.” Hardware notes on the same page: extra tile retention and plasma-path fixes drawn from Flight 13 debris in the Indian Ocean; curved tiles in selected areas; two tiles recovered from Ship 40 planned to fly again on Ship 41 (first announced tile reuse). Ship will attempt orbital insertion only after the flight-control team confirms deorbit-burn redundancy. Reuters, Space.com, TechCrunch and Ars Technica all matched the company page. Elon Musk quote-posted the SpaceX announcement: “Next Starship flight on 9/22.” Sources: SpaceX launch page; @SpaceX; Reuters; Space.com; Ars Technica; TechCrunch.

### Satellite broadband — Starlink V3 as Flight 14 payload
**CONFIRMED.** The Flight 14 page says Starship will deploy 26 Starlink V3 satellites, the first V3 batch. SpaceX states each V3 adds 1 Tbps of constellation capacity (26 Tbps this flight), about 10× a Falcon 9 V2 Mini launch. After deploy the satellites unfold antennas and solar arrays, check in over RF and laser links, then raise orbit; customer service is described as possible within weeks of checkout. Three satellites carry cameras to image Starship’s heat shield. Musk, quoting Sawyer Merritt’s recap of the official page, wrote: “Starlink V3 constellation starts being deployed this month. Ultimately, it will deliver more than 100 times the bandwidth of our current 11,000 satellite constellation.” Treat the 100× figure as an Elon claim layered on the company’s 1 Tbps-per-sat spec, not an independent measurement. Sources: SpaceX Flight 14 page; @elonmusk; Sawyer Merritt.
