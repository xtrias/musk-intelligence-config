### NHTSA — Audit Query AQ26002 on Cybercab self-certification
**CONFIRMED (primary documents).** NHTSA Office of Defects Investigation opened Audit Query AQ26002 on 3 September 2026, "prompted by public information." Population: 1,000 vehicles estimated. Subject: Tesla Cybercab FMVSS certification. Investigator Syed Rahaman; reviewer Neil Dold; approver Otto Matheke. Tesla notified the agency it had certified the Austin Cybercabs as compliant with all applicable Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards and that it plans to expand to additional vehicles and locations. The vehicles "lack permanently attached, conventional manual controls, such as a brake pedal, gas pedal, steering wheel, and mirrors." NHTSA will examine the process and technical data behind that certification, "including the extent to which Tesla's certification depended on determinations that certain FMVSS are inapplicable to the Cybercab." Tesla had not filed an FMVSS exemption request (Zoox did, after an earlier self-certification episode, and received a limited exemption in July 2026). This is an audit of the certification basis, not a finding that the vehicles are defective. Tesla had no immediate comment.

NHTSA Administrator Jonathan Morrison, 4 September press release: "NHTSA fully supports the safe development and deployment of automated vehicles. But as the federal regulator, we need to ensure that all of our laws are followed. Our approach of balancing innovation with safety oversight will allow the United States to maintain its global leadership in AV innovation." The same release notes NHTSA is rewriting eight AV-related standards (brake pedals, wipers, lighting, mirrors among them) but that existing FMVSS remain in force until those rulemakings finish.

### xAI / SpaceXAI — Minnesota injunction denied; Memphis outage
**CONFIRMED.** U.S. District Judge Donovan Frank (D. Minn.) on 4 September denied xAI's motion for a preliminary injunction against Minnesota's HF 1606 "nudification" statute (Minn. Stat. § 325E.91), in force since 1 August. Case: X.AI LLC v. Ellison, No. 0:26-cv-03425. The law bars operators from letting users "nudify" images of identifiable people with AI; civil penalties run up to $500,000 per violation. Frank held xAI had not shown irreparable harm and that waiting until days before the effective date weighed against emergency relief. The constitutional case continues. Attorney General Keith Ellison: the office will keep defending the law. xAI and the AG's office did not immediately comment to Reuters. https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/musks-xai-loses-court-bid-block-minnesotas-ai-nudification-ban-2026-09-04/ https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/09/04/judge-lets-minnesota-enforce-antinudification-app-law-over-xai-objection-as-case-proceeds https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.mnd.235231/gov.uscourts.mnd.235231.54.0.pdf
