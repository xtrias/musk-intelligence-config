### Orbital launch — Starship Flight 14 slips six days
**CONFIRMED.** SpaceX moved Starship Flight 14 from Tuesday 22 September to “as early as Monday, September 28, pending regulatory approval.” The official account posted the change at 17:10 UTC on 17 September; the company launch page now lists a 75-minute window opening 7:15 a.m. CT (08:15–09:30 ET). Space.com said SpaceX gave no explanation. NASASpaceflight, LabPadre and StarshipGazer independently logged updated NOTAMs / NOTMARs that start 28 September.

The mission profile is otherwise unchanged from the 15 September brief: first planned orbital insertion of Starship (Ship 41 / Super Heavy B21, Block 3), about six orbits at ~275 km, nearly 10 hours, splashdown in the Pacific west of Chile. Booster aims for a controlled Gulf splashdown; no tower catch on this flight. Payload is Starlink Group 31-1: 26 production V3 satellites, three of them camera-equipped to image the heat shield. SpaceX’s page states each V3 adds 1 Tbps of constellation capacity, “26 Tbps … on this mission alone.” Flight 13’s landing-burn ice-clog on three center engines remains the hardware context; Flight 14 adds engine filtering and relight-software changes. FAA/AST licensing is still the gate. Sources: @SpaceX; SpaceX Flight 14 page; Space.com; NASASpaceflight; LabPadre.

### Orbital launch — USSF-259 flies from Vandenberg
**CONFIRMED.** After a 15 September stand-down before fueling, Falcon 9 lifted USSF-259 from Vandenberg SLC-4E at 18:07:56 PDT on 16 September (01:07:56 UTC 17 September). Booster B1097 flew for the 13th time and landed on droneship *Of Course I Still Love You*. The payload is a classified U.S. Space Force mission under NSSL Phase 3 Lane 1 — the second classified USSF launch in under a week after USSF-153 on 10 September. SpaceX posted liftoff, landing and pad photos. NASASpaceflight counted it as Falcon 9 flight 689 and SpaceX’s 110th orbital launch of 2026 (watcher tally, not a company scoreboard). Sources: @SpaceX; SpaceX USSF-259 page; Spaceflight Now; NASASpaceflight.

### Autonomy / regulation — Cybercab Special Order still the live federal action
**CONFIRMED (continuing).** NHTSA’s Special Order inside Audit Query AQ26002, signed 10 September and reported by Reuters on 15 September, remains the binding document. Tesla must deliver sworn answers to 21 questions by 30 September on how it self-certified a wheel-less, pedal-less, mirror-less Cybercab under existing FMVSS. Request 19 points at FMVSS No. 135 S5.3.1 (“The service brakes shall be activated by means of a foot control”). Other requests cover temporary human controls, the Safety Act “make inoperative” rule, FMVSS 101/102/108/111/126, and why Tesla certified without a Part 555 exemption. Yahoo Finance, IBD, Motor1 and Drive Tesla Canada recirculated the order on 16–17 September. Civil-penalty language cited by those desks (up to $27,874 per day, capped near $139 million) is a statutory reading, not a fine levied. Tesla has not published a reply. Austin paid Cybercab service, begun 3 September, continues while the audit runs. Sources: Reuters 15 September; NHTSA ODI PDF INIM-AQ26002-17090P1; Yahoo Finance; IBD.

### Heavy-duty charging — Tesla to run public Megachargers at Forum Mobility depots
**CONFIRMED.** Reuters, 17 September: Tesla will operate public Megacharging sites at three new Forum Mobility electric-truck depots in California — FM Francis in Ontario and FM Adeline and FM Coliseum in Oakland — as Forum adds 30 MW of heavy-vehicle capacity. Sites mix Megawatt Charging System ports with CCS and use pull-through lanes. Forum said its FM Santa Fe depot in Rancho Dominguez already has fleet commitments for more than 330 Tesla Semis and will also serve other carriers. Two MCS Megachargers were added at FM Harbor, Port of Long Beach (operational since December 2024). Electrek framed the model as Tesla running chargers on a partner’s land rather than building every stall itself. Why it matters: Semi utilization is gated by high-power charging on freight corridors, not only by the Nevada plant. Sources: Reuters; Electrek; Forum Mobility via PR desks.

### Chip manufacturing — Tesla and SpaceX sue over the “Terafab” name
**CONFIRMED.** Reuters (16 September) reported Tesla and SpaceX filed in the U.S. District Court for the Western District of Texas, Austin, case 1:26-cv-02543, seeking a declaration that “Terafab” does not infringe TERA-print LLC’s federally registered “Tera-Fab” mark. Tesla filed three USPTO applications in May; TERA-print sent a cease-and-desist on 23 May and later threatened suit. TERA-print said on Wednesday that settlement talks ran as recently as 2 September and that it will defend the mark. The complaint restates the previously announced Grimes County plant: initial ~$16.8 billion to make, package and test advanced logic and memory under one roof for Optimus, Cybercab and SpaceX orbital compute. Counsel named by Reuters: Andrei Iancu (Sullivan & Cromwell) for Tesla; Megan Bannigan (Debevoise) for SpaceX. This is a trademark fight, not a new construction permit. Sources: Reuters; Teslarati.
