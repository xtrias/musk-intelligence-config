**CONFIRMED.** Official @teslaeurope, 07:40 CEST 1 Sep, plus a same-day Reuters write-through from Stockholm: FSD Supervised is approved in five EU states — Netherlands, Denmark, Belgium, Estonia, Lithuania — used by more than 70,000 customers for over 1 million km per day. Tesla says vehicles on FSD were 4.1× less likely to crash than Tesla manual driving across more than 100 million km on public EU roads between April and August. Reuters published the raw counts Tesla supplied: FSD-equipped cars were in 3 highway and 9 non-highway collisions; manually driven Teslas in the same markets were in 137 highway and 490 non-highway collisions. Tesla open-sourced the Article 39 dashboard previously given to member states after the Dutch RDW approval, ahead of a possible Technical Committee on Motor Vehicles vote on 6 October 2026. Design was adjusted to UN-R-171 DCAS; Tesla sought Article 39 exemptions for some behaviors. The 4.1× multiple is Tesla’s comparison against its own manual-driven fleet, not an independent regulator study. Drive Tesla Canada notes Tesla’s July figure on a smaller sample was 5.2×, so the newer claim covers more kilometres at a lower multiple. Independent FSD-EU-Tracker: 5 of 27 states, about 8.9% of EU population; bloc-wide recognition needs 15 states and 65% of population. Reuters separately recalled its earlier reporting that experts have called Tesla’s published safety comparisons invalid.
