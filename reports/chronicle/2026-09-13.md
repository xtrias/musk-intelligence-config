### SpaceX — Falcon 9 flies the last three SES O3b mPOWER satellites; Flight 14 pad cleared
**CONFIRMED.** SpaceX launched Falcon 9 from SLC-40 at Cape Canaveral at 18:49 UTC Sunday (window open 14:49 ET) with SES O3b mPOWER satellites F11, F12 and F13 bound for medium Earth orbit. The company posted “Liftoff!” then, nine minutes later, “Falcon 9’s first stage has landed on the A Shortfall of Gravitas droneship.” Booster B1080 flew its 29th mission after a 61-day turnaround (prior flights include Ax-2, Euclid, Ax-3, CRS-30, SES ASTRA 1P, NG-21 and 22 Starlink missions). NASASpaceflight counted the flight as SpaceX’s 109th orbital launch of 2026 and the fourth of September. Weather was a watch item: the 45th Weather Squadron had 60% favorable odds at window open, falling to 40% later on sea-breeze storms; SpaceX went at T0.

This was the first three-satellite mPOWER flight and the last of the 13-spacecraft Boeing 702X constellation. Spaceflight Now: each satellite has a dry mass of about 1,900 kg; early spacecraft had power-module issues, sats 7–11 were upgraded, and 12–13 were added. SES has said the new birds enter service mid-2027. SpaceX’s published timeline put deployments at T+33:41 / T+40:41 / T+47:41 (~19:23–19:37 UTC). At desk cutoff SpaceX and SES had confirmed ascent and booster recovery only — not the three deployments. No Reuters or SpaceNews same-day wrap was found.
